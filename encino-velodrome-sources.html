01 · Read this first

Two things are rotting in public

Neither is anyone's fault. Both are fixable this month, and both get harder the longer they sit.

The entire results archive is already gone

The Results page lists 27 race meetings from 2009 to 2014, each linking to a PDF or spreadsheet. I pulled all 39 file links and requested every one of them. All 39 return 404. The page looks like an archive and functions as a dead end: a visitor clicking any result from any year gets nothing.

The files did not move, they stopped being served when the site changed hands. Which means the only surviving copies are in Vinh's repo or the Internet Archive.

FIX → the repo copy answers this. Failing that, the Wayback Machine is blocked from my environment but not from yours: pull web.archive.org/web/2015*/encinovelodrome.org/eventresults/*. I have saved the complete list of all 39 filenames so nothing gets missed.

The old site is still live, and it is the better site

The previous WordPress site never came down. It sits at encinovelodrome.wordpress.com, it is fully readable, and it contains the club's entire written library: the founding history, Bob Hansing's full obituary, a seven-step Beginner's Guide, Rider Etiquette, a Code of Conduct, a complete Track Racing FAQ explaining every event on a velodrome, the Youth Program, weekly training, sponsorship tiers, and the track records board.

Almost none of this exists on the current site. It is a free WordPress.com site tied to an account nobody has mentioned, which means it can vanish without notice and take the only public copy of the records with it.

FIX → I have already mirrored every page of it to text. The originals should be exported properly and the good material republished under the club's own domain.

The campaign

"The board is half empty"

Every women's record at this velodrome is unclaimed. Whether that is a record-keeping gap or the literal truth, it is the most inviting fact on the entire site: show up, ride a Flying 200, and your name is the first one on the board.

Verify with the board first. If any women's times exist on paper, the story becomes restoring them, which is just as good.

The hook

The Track School carousel already explains the Flying 200 world record. Now it has a local anchor: the fastest ever ridden on this concrete is Kevin Mansker's 11.18 from 2011, still standing after fifteen years.

Why it matters

Personality is the differentiator

Carson has the Olympic track. Encino has Attack of the Track People. No amount of budget buys that tone, and reviving it costs nothing but nerve. A named race with a poster is a content series, a ticket driver, and a reason for press to care.

The thread

The Hansing series

Season-long standings ran under the Hansing name, after founder Bob Hansing. One of the 2010 races was presented by Euro Asia Imports, the company Hansing himself founded. The club has been honoring its own history in its race calendar the whole time.

Old sponsors

A warm list, not a cold one

Privateer, Orange 20 Bikes, South Bay Wheelmen, Zone 3 Multisport, LÄRABAR, Euro Asia Imports, Saguaro Velo. These businesses backed races here before. That is the first call list for sponsorship, and it is not a cold one.

04 · The story

A golf game, a flood basin, and a man who found Japan

The founding story is fully documented and genuinely good. It has a beginning, a turn, and a punchline, which is more than most brands can say.

1953

"What if we build our own velodrome?"

Four friends in the bike trade — George Garner, Bob Hansing, Jack Kemp, Charlie Morton — on a ride, half joking. Later that year Morton plays golf with a friend from the Army Corps of Engineers and hears about an unused piece of land in the Sepulveda flood basin.

They form a nonprofit, raise money from the cycling trade, and each put in their own. Asphalt for the 1961 season, cement by 1963.

The punchline

The first man to ride it

The grading finishes. Three of them stand admiring the work, look up, and Hansing is already out there riding laps on the bare dirt oval. He went into the record as the first person ever to ride the Encino Velodrome.

The circle

Hansing found the track in Japan

Navy, Korea, early 1950s. Ashore at Yokohama and Sasebo, in the club's own words: "While others headed for the bars Bob headed for the nearest Japanese velodrome." He borrowed a bike far too small for him and rode the oriental oval.

Seventy years later the club's treasurer, Henry Shibata, is a Japanese keirin rider who pilots the pace scooter. The founder went to Japan to find keirin; keirin came to Encino. That is the best story this place has and nobody has told it.

Hansing, in full

His obituary is on the old site and reads like a history of American cycling: Montrose Bike Shop, opened 1955 and family-run for 46 years. Founder of Euro Asia Imports. President of Shimano American Corporation. Founder of the Montrose Cycle Club, which produced national and Olympic riders. Fifteen years on the board of the Amateur Bicycle League of America. Coach of the 1968 US Olympic cycling team in Mexico City, manager of the 1971 Pan-American team in Cali. He announced the races at Encino for years while his wife Bev worked the concession stand. Born Glendale 1931, died 2001.

05 · What else is out there

Assets, names and numbers

Audio

A narrated history, already recorded

Sitting unlinked on the live site: an 8.4 MB MP3 of the Velodrome's history, narrated by Roger Espinosa. I have downloaded it. That is a podcast episode, a reel voiceover bed, and an audiogram series with no production cost.

Video

A YouTube channel and a playlist

youtube.com/c/EncinoVelodrome, plus an Encino Velodrome Videos playlist and third-party coverage. Pure Cycles filmed the Swap Your Legs race. There is also a run of remote-control car racing videos shot on the track, including an 83 mph RC speed run, which is a genuinely odd and very shareable side of the place.

Photos

A Flickr archive, referenced

The old site embeds a Flickr feed and a Photo Archive page that now 404s. Finding who holds that Flickr account is a priority, it is likely the largest photo set in existence and it belongs in the asset drive.

People worth chasing

  • The board, on record: Roger Espinosa (President), Rick Skalak (VP), Henry Shibata (Treasurer), William J. Smith (Secretary/Grants), Vinh Tran (Technology), Davin Agustines, Todd Tuengel, Jane Skalak, Rob Bush.
  • Rick Denman founded the youth program here, free coaching and equipment for anyone eighteen and under. Worth a profile and worth knowing whether that program still runs in that form.
  • "Dingo" ran Monday evening structured training and got a headline on the old site when he came back to it. Still on the schedule today as the Monday session. A nickname with that much staying power is a character.
  • Durer Shomer wrote the club's track racing FAQ, which is the best explainer of track cycling I have read on any club site.
  • John Fitzpatrick and Dave Staub have a memorial championship named for them. I could not find who they were, and someone at the track will know.

Numbers to keep straight

  • 250 metres, concrete, banked at 46 degrees, which makes it one of the steepest tracks anywhere. Riders aged 9 to 80+.
  • $10 a session, $40 beginner certification, $5 bike rental. Annual membership $250, with $75 and $50 tiers for people who put work into the place.
  • Club track rental $100/hour, two-hour minimum, plus 50% if the lights are on.
  • Donation tiers run Supporter, Friend, Soigneur, Medalist, Hall of Fame at $3,000, and donors can earmark a specific project: the motor, the boards, the lights, the concrete, the landscaping.
  • Club races are sanctioned through ATRA, not USAC. That matters for the results system: it means the club is free to publish its own standings without a national body in the loop.
  • The rebuild spec names every line to be repainted: cote d'azur, black measurement, sprinter's red, stayer's blue, 200m, two pursuit lines, finish. The brand palette and the concrete agree with each other.

06 · What this changes

Six things I would do with this

  1. Rescue the results archive before anything else. Repo first, Wayback second. I have the full filename list ready.
  2. Export the old WordPress site properly and republish the FAQ, Beginner's Guide, Etiquette and history under the club's own domain. Track School is no longer something I have to write, it is something the club already wrote and forgot.
  3. Run "the board is half empty" as the first real campaign, once the board confirms the women's records are genuinely open. It is the strongest invitation this place has.
  4. Tell the Hansing and Shibata story. A founder who went looking for velodromes in postwar Japan, and a Japanese keirin rider running the scooter today. That is the anchor piece for the whole heritage pillar.
  5. Bring back the race names. Put a real one on the 2027 calendar with a poster, and let the tone do the marketing.
  6. Call the old sponsors. Privateer, Orange 20, South Bay Wheelmen, Zone 3, Euro Asia. They backed races here before, and the rebuild gives them a reason to come back.
