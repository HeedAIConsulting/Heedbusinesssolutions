01 · Why now

A national-caliber story, told at club volume

This track hosted the 1965 and 1968 US National Championships, the 1968 US Olympic Trials, and the 1972 and 1973 Grand Prix of the United States on ABC television. Most of the Valley drives past it without knowing it exists. That gap between the story and its reach is exactly what social media is for.

The secret spot

LA loves discovering places like this

A 65-year-old concrete velodrome behind a chain-link fence is precisely the kind of place LA content thrives on. Real banking, real motorpacing, no-brakes track bikes, and a $10 price of entry make it endlessly shareable.

Built-in shot list

The calendar already makes the content

Training six days a week, free junior sessions on Thursdays, Keirin drills on Saturday mornings, time trials and race days through the season, plus swap meets and a 2026 club kit. I never have to invent a reason to post.

The rebuild

A fundraising campaign that needs an audience

Restoring Turns 1–4 with reinforced shotcrete is estimated at $875k–$950k. Donor campaigns run on story and momentum. A living feed that shows the track loved and used is the strongest case for writing a check.

One honest caveat: Instagram restricts profile data for logged-out review, so a full audit of the current account (followers, reach, posting history, best performers) is step one the moment I get collaborator access. Everything in this proposal was built from the Velodrome's public website, photo library, event listings, and published history.

02 · The strategy

Three audiences, five pillars

Every post serves one of three people: the rider-to-be who doesn't yet know the track welcomes beginners, the neighbor and enthusiast who wants somewhere genuinely cool to be on a race night, and the cycling community and donor who will carry the rebuild. The content mix stays honest to all three.

The Wall
~30% · Reels-first

Spectacle plus smarts. POV laps, motorpacing, banking shots, and the Track School explainer series. The scroll-stopper that makes strangers ask "where IS that?"

Start Here
~25% · The funnel

The beginner path, repeated forever: $40 certification class, $5 rental bikes, $10 sessions. Every objection answered, link in bio.

Race Night
~20% · Events

Promos, live stories, results and podiums for race days and time trials. Positioned as a thing to do in LA, free to watch.

Since '61
~10% · Heritage

Archive photos, Nationals and Olympic Trials history, founder stories. The moat no other LA venue can copy. Feeds kit sales and donations.

Track Family
~15% · Community

Faces of the track, volunteers, free junior Thursdays, swap meets. Proof that this is a club with a door wide open.

What it is
A time trial with a running start

You get two or three laps to wind up, climbing high on the banking. Then the clock catches your final 200 meters: dive off the wall, hug the black line, empty the tank.

2 / 6
Why it exists
Ten seconds that seed the sprint

Before any match sprint tournament, every rider gets one Flying 200. The times set the bracket: fastest faces slowest in round one.

One flat-out effort decides your whole day. No teammates, no draft, nowhere to hide.

3 / 6
Since when
Seventy years of chasing tenths

Sprinting is track cycling's original discipline, and ratified Flying 200 records reach back to 1954, when Italy's Cesare Pinarello clocked 12.0 at Milan's legendary Vigorelli.

Aero tucks, harder gears and faster tracks later, we are under nine.

encinovelodrome Why is there a motorcycle on the track? Physics. 🏍️

Above 30 mph, most of what you fight is air. Tucked behind the moto, that wall of wind disappears, so you can hold race speed long enough for your legs to learn it.

That is motorpacing: leg speed, positioning, and confidence at speeds you cannot reach alone. And it happens right here in Encino.

Ask us about structured training. Link in bio.

#Motorpacing #TrackCycling #TrainFast #EncinoVelodrome #BikeLA

Why it works: answers a question every visitor actually asks and positions the Velodrome as coaching, not just concrete. The pilot is Henry Shibata, and post 06 tells his story.

If you have trained here, you know the hum of Henry's scooter before you see it. He has given years to this velodrome, keeps the books as club treasurer, and pilots the pace scooter that teaches our riders to hold real speed.

And here is the part that makes visitors do a double take: Henry raced keirin in Japan, where the sport was born and where pacing riders is an art of its own. When you follow his wheel around our banking, you are learning from the real thing.

Find Henry at Keirin training, Saturdays 9am to noon. Say hi. Ask him anything.

  • Beginner class signups (tracked link)
