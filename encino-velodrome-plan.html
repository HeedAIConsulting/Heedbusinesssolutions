Hold your line.

The pitch is done. This is the build. Three things start now: a content engine that runs every week without anyone having to think of a post, an asset drive to recover the Velodrome's history before it disappears, and a website rebuilt around the one thing the current site cannot do: publish race results.

01 · Where it stands

The gap is not effort. It's plumbing.

The club does the hard part already: six days of training a week, free junior sessions, Keirin mornings, a full race calendar. What is missing is the machinery that turns all that activity into something the public can see, follow, and act on.

The evidence

The results page stops in July 2014

Twelve years of racing has happened since, and none of it is on the site. What is there sits in PDFs and Excel files, one download at a time. A rider who raced last month has nothing to look up, share, or be proud of.

The cost

Every race night evaporates

No standings means no reason to come back next week to defend a place. No records board means a fast Flying 200 goes unrecognized. No rider pages means nothing to send to a parent, a coach, or a sponsor.

The opportunity

Results are the best content there is

They are already produced, they carry names, and people search for their own name. Publishing them well feeds the website, the social feed, and the fundraising case at the same time, from one act of data entry.

04 · The asset drive

Get the history off the hard drives

Sixty-five seasons of photos, results and stories exist mostly in private hands: shoeboxes, garages, dead laptops. Publicly available material I can scrape. Everything else has to be asked for, and asked for now, while the people who have it are still around to ask.

Collect

Photos, reports, legends, records

Race and event photos, race reports and old result sheets, the local legends and what they did, record holders, and anything else specific to Encino. Unsorted and unlabeled is fine.

Where it goes

Four places at once

The Since '61 content pillar, the new site's history and archive pages, the all-time records board, and the rebuild campaign, where proof of what this place was is the strongest argument for what it can be.

How

One email, one destination

The ask goes out to everyone connected to the track, with a single upload link so nothing gets lost in reply chains. Every contributor is credited by name when their material runs.

One decision needed: where submissions land. The cleanest answer is an upload page on the new site that writes straight into the archive, which means the drive and the rebuild should start together rather than in sequence. A shared Drive folder works as a stopgap if the email needs to go out first.

05 · The website

Rebuilt around race results

The current site is a Zoho Sites build: fine for a brochure, structurally unable to run results. The rebuild keeps what works, sharpens the path for new riders, and adds the thing the club has never had: a way for a coordinator to put results up the same night they are ridden. With the domain on GoDaddy, the cutover is a DNS change, and email stays untouched on Zoho.

Race night, end to end

Modeled on how OnTheDay.net works for road promoters, where results go online immediately after data entry and standings update themselves. Adapted for a weekly club track night, which has many short events and no pre-entry database.

Coordinator opens the night

Logs in, picks the date, and chooses events from a template: scratch, points, miss-and-out, keirin, handicap, Flying 200, 500m, kilo, team sprint. Categories come preset (A/B/C, juniors, masters, women) and are editable per event.

Enters results, two ways

Either type them in on a phone or tablet at trackside, with rider names autocompleting from the rider table, or paste and upload the spreadsheet the club already keeps on a clipboard. Both paths matter: nobody should have to change how they work on race night.

Saves as a draft, then publishes

Nothing is public until the coordinator hits publish, so a half-entered night never leaks. Entry keeps working with bad signal in the Basin and syncs when it reconnects.

The site does the rest

One publish updates the results page, recalculates season standings, refreshes each rider profile, and checks every time against the records board, flagging a new club record automatically.

The feed gets fed

The same publish generates a ready-to-post results graphic in the club's colors and a printable PDF for the noticeboard. Race night becomes Sunday's post with no extra work.

What this deliberately does not do
  • USA Cycling submission and national rankings. OnTheDay.net already does this for sanctioned events and does it properly. For sanctioned race days the club keeps using it, and the site links or mirrors those results rather than competing with them.
  • Chip timing or hardware. Times come from the people already holding the stopwatches.
  • Race registration and payment. BikeReg handles that today and works. The site links out.
