Wix vs. a Custom Website: What Every Law Firm Needs to Know

Law firms compete on trust, credibility, and the ability to communicate expertise before a conversation ever happens. This is a direct comparison of Wix and a custom-built website across every dimension that matters to a professional service firm.

Let us be direct about what this comparison is and is not. This is not a debate about whether Wix can produce a functional website — it can. This is a rigorous look at how Wix performs against a professionally built custom site across the specific criteria that determine whether a law firm's website is a liability or an asset. For a firm where a single matter can be worth $5,000 to $50,000 or more, the difference between a site that converts prospects and one that merely exists is a material business outcome. The comparison deserves to be made clearly and honestly.

To make the comparison concrete, consider a mid-size family law firm in Los Angeles — six attorneys, three practice areas (divorce, custody, and property division), a mix of high-net-worth and middle-market clients, and a strong referral network they are actively trying to grow. They need a website that ranks in local search, builds immediate credibility with prospective clients who may be in a highly emotional state when they first make contact, handles consultation scheduling, and performs well enough technically to support any paid advertising they run. Here is how Wix and a custom site compare on each of those requirements.

The Head-to-Head: Eight Criteria That Determine Website Value for Law Firms

SEO Performance. Wix has made genuine improvements to its SEO tools, but the platform's JavaScript architecture creates structural disadvantages — slower Core Web Vitals, limited schema customization, and URL structures that have historically been less clean than static or server-rendered alternatives. A custom HTML site, built with clean semantic markup, hand-coded LegalService schema, and a CDN-backed hosting setup, will consistently outperform a Wix site on the technical signals that Google uses to rank local professional service pages. For a competitive market like Los Angeles family law, those technical advantages compound into meaningful ranking differences over 12 to 24 months.

Load Speed. On mobile — where the majority of local legal searches originate — Wix sites routinely score in the "needs improvement" range on Google's PageSpeed Insights due to the platform's JavaScript overhead. Custom sites built and optimized by professionals regularly score 90 or above on the same tool. Every second of load time above 2.5 seconds correlates with measurably higher bounce rates. For a prospect who is already anxious about their legal situation and evaluating multiple firms simultaneously, a slow site is not just an inconvenience — it is a reason to leave.

Design Control. Wix offers visual flexibility within its template and component system, but that system imposes a ceiling. Custom CSS is possible but limited. Typography control is constrained to Wix's font library and weighting options. Layout structures outside the template's grid require workarounds. A custom-built site has no such ceiling — every pixel, every spacing value, every color, every interaction behavior is specified and built to the firm's exact requirements. For a law firm that wants to convey authority, precision, and attention to detail, that ceiling matters.

Conversion Rate. Conversion rate optimization — the discipline of structuring a site to maximize the percentage of visitors who take a desired action — is fundamentally constrained on Wix by template structure. A custom site can be built from the ground up around conversion: a hero section that leads with the client's problem, social proof positioned at the precise moment of hesitation, a booking flow that minimizes friction, and a mobile experience that makes calling or scheduling as simple as one tap. These are architecture decisions, not content decisions, and they are only fully available on a custom build.

Long-Term Cost. Over three years, a fully equipped Wix site for a law firm — Business plan plus Bookings plus email marketing plus any third-party apps needed for intake management and CRM integration — easily reaches $2,500 to $4,000 in platform fees alone, without producing an asset the firm owns. A custom site represents a higher upfront investment that, amortized over the same period, often costs less than or equal to the Wix stack — and leaves the firm with an owned digital asset, not a rented one.

  • SEO performance: Custom site wins on technical architecture and schema control
  • Mobile load speed: Custom site consistently achieves sub-2-second LCP; Wix averages 3.5 to 5 seconds
  • Design control: Custom site has no ceiling; Wix is constrained by template and component system
  • Conversion rate: Custom site built around conversion goals; Wix built around general visual flexibility
  • Long-term cost: Custom site often comparable or lower over 3 years when Wix add-ons are fully accounted for
  • Ownership: Custom site is a firm-owned asset; Wix is a rented presence on a closed platform
  • Professional credibility: Custom site signals investment; Wix signals convenience and speed
  • Security: Custom sites on reputable hosting have direct control over security headers, SSL, and access; Wix controls these at the platform level

The Verdict for Professional Service Firms

There are contexts where Wix makes sense — a very early stage solo practitioner who needs any online presence while building a client base, a professional who genuinely has no near-term budget for a custom build, or a firm that is planning a full rebrand in six months and needs a temporary placeholder. These are real use cases, and Wix serves them reasonably well.

But for an established law firm competing in a market as sophisticated and competitive as Los Angeles — or any major metropolitan area — Wix is a strategic liability. It limits SEO performance at exactly the moment when search visibility is a primary client acquisition channel. It limits design quality at exactly the moment when first impressions determine whether a prospect converts. It limits technical capability at exactly the moment when integration with intake management, scheduling, and CRM systems is essential for operational efficiency. And it communicates the wrong message at exactly the moment when the message most needs to be right.

The firms that consistently win new clients from search, sustain strong referral networks, and convert first-time visitors into scheduled consultations are not the ones with the most keywords on their pages. They are the ones who invested in building a digital presence that matches the quality of the work they do. A custom site is that investment. For most professional service firms evaluating the question honestly, it is also the smarter financial decision over any realistic time horizon.
