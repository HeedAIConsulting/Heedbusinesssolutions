02 · Folder 1

Friday night, 28 August

The folder is named 2026-08-29. The photographs were not taken on the 29th. Every file carries two timestamps: the moment the shutter fired and the moment Lightroom wrote the export. The export stamps are all 29 August, 16:29. The shutter stamps are all Friday 28 August, between 18:10 and 19:29. These are pictures of a Friday night session, taken two weeks ago.

Read in order, the exposures tell you the whole evening without anyone having to remember it. Wide 28mm on the apron at ISO 250 while the sun is still up. Then eighty minutes on a 135mm at f/1.8 through the golden hour. Then, at 19:12, the ISO jumps to 6400 — the floodlights are on and the daylight is gone. And at 19:25 the shutter drops to 1/80, which no one does by accident: those last three frames are panning shots, the photographer tracking a rider so the background tears into streaks.

03 · Folder 2

The morning set

Eight RAW files from a Sony a6000, shot with a fully manual lens — the file records the lens model as ----, which is what a camera writes when nothing electronic is attached. Times run 06:47 to 08:12, and the light in the frames agrees: hard low sun, shadows running half the width of the track.

The date on this folder cannot be right, and I cannot tell you what is

Vinh filed these as 2025. The camera wrote 31 October 2018 into every file. Those cannot both be true, and there is nothing else in the files to break the tie — no GPS, no lens electronics, no software stamp.

The times themselves are internally consistent, so the clock was not simply reset to a default; either it is right and the folder name is a guess, or the camera's clock was wrong by years and the folder name is correct. One question to whoever shot them settles it. Until it is settled these cannot be captioned with a date or tied to an event.

Trackside

IMG_0838.MOV

3.7 s · 2160×3840 · HEVC · 30 fps
Fri 9 Feb 2024, 20:24

iPhone 14 Pro, vertical, shot from the rail as two riders come past under lights. Under four seconds — too short to be a post on its own, exactly right as the opening or closing beat of one.

Why the vertical part matters

Reels, Stories and TikTok are 9:16. Landscape footage cropped to 9:16 loses two-thirds of the frame, and on a track — where the interest is the banking angle and the rider's line — that crop usually throws away the reason the shot worked. Two of these three clips were filmed vertical. They can be cut and posted without losing anything. The motorpacing clip is landscape, but the rider holds the centre of frame the whole way through, so it is one of the few landscape clips that crops cleanly.

05 · The find

The file called aaamockup‑2.pdf

A name like that gets skipped. It is a Photoshop-wrapped photograph, written on 3 November 2024, and it is the most directly useful single image anyone has handed me.

Why it matters

This is the proof, not the poster

Read the jerseys. There are California Republic state champion's jerseys from both 2023 and 2024, more than one of them. There is a jersey carrying UCI rainbow bands. There is a stars-and-stripes national champion's jersey.

A club that has to argue it is worth joining is in a weak position. A club that can show a photograph of its own members in champion's jerseys, standing on its own steps, does not have to argue.

Before it goes anywhere

Three things to fix

The source. What I have is a JPEG inside a PDF. Someone has the original camera file and it will be sharper. Worth asking for.

The handrail. There is a sticker on the rail running directly beneath the front row. It has been applied back-to-front, and read the other way it is a word the club will not want under a group photograph. It needs cloning out.

The names. Twenty identifiable people. Before this is used publicly the club needs to know who they are, so that the champions can be named and anyone who would rather not appear can be left out.

Nothing has been retouched, cropped or watermarked; this is an inventory, not a portfolio, and the originals stay untouched.

07 · Gaps

What is missing

A fair summary: one very good Friday evening, one undated morning, three clips, and one group photograph. That will carry roughly six to eight weeks of posting. It will not carry a season, and there are specific holes.

Nobody in the stands

No crowd, anywhere

Twenty-nine images and not one spectator, official, or family on the grandstand. The club photograph is the only frame with a group of people in it. Anyone deciding whether to come and watch has nothing to look at.

No juniors

Not one rider under about thirty

The archive shows junior racing at this track for decades. The photo library has no evidence of it. That is a problem for a club that wants families.

No women's racing

Two frames, both solo

There are women in the library and one of them is in a rainbow-band jersey. There is no photograph of a women's field racing. The results archive says those races happened.

Nothing before 2018

No history at all

The track opened in 1961. The oldest file here is seven years old at most. Every anniversary post, every "then and now", every obituary and tribute needs material nobody has yet sent.

No beginners' session

The thing we most need to sell

One frame — a rider in a t-shirt — carries the entire "you can just turn up" message. It should be twenty frames and a clip of someone's first lap.

No credit line

Nobody's name is on anything

Not one of the twenty-five files carries an Artist or Copyright field. See below — this is the one that blocks publishing.

08 · Rights

Credit

Nothing here can be posted publicly until we know who took it

I checked every one of the twenty-five files for an Artist or Copyright field. All twenty-five are empty. The a9 files went through Lightroom, so the photographer had an obvious opportunity to stamp their name and did not take it — which usually means these were shared informally between friends, not licensed.

That is completely normal for a volunteer club and it is still a problem. Photographs belong to the person who took them. Posting them on the club's accounts without asking is how a club loses a photographer, and photographers are the hardest volunteers to replace.

What I need is small: a name per folder, and a yes. In exchange every photographer gets a credit on every post their work appears in, and a standing invitation to shoot. That is the entire ask, and it is the first paragraph of the email going out to the wider list.

Where the rest comes from

There is now a shared Drive folder that anyone can upload to without needing an account or a login, and an email going out to photographers, race organisers and long-standing volunteers pointing at it. The ask is deliberately low-effort: drop files in, no sorting, no renaming, no captions. Sorting is my job and I would rather have a messy folder than an empty one. What comes back gets read, catalogued and added to this page exactly the way this batch was.

How this was produced. All twenty-five files were downloaded in full from the shared Drive folders and checksummed.
